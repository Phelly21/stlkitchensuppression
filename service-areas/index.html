Short answer: We accept requests from St. Louis City, St. Louis County, St. Charles County in Missouri, and the Metro East in Illinois. The service your hood system needs is the same everywhere, but who enforces the fire code, which edition applies, and what permits look like differ by region and sometimes by fire district.

Why Your Region Matters

A kitchen hood suppression system needs the same core care wherever it is: service at least every six months and after any discharge, working fuel or power shutoff, clean and capped nozzles, and current fusible links. What changes from place to place is the authority having jurisdiction (AHJ), the office that reviews plans, witnesses acceptance tests and writes up deficiencies.

Missouri has no statewide fire code for restaurants, so cities and fire protection districts adopt their own. Illinois is different: the state adopted a Life Safety Code that applies everywhere, and it licenses the technicians who service kitchen systems. That's why a remodel in Wildwood, a new build in Wentzville, and an upgrade in Belleville can follow three different sets of steps.

What Every Region Has in Common

Whatever your region, the common requests are the same: a semiannual kitchen fire suppression inspection, a recharge or repair after a discharge or failed test, fixes for fire code deficiencies, and new systems and UL 300 upgrades. Before an inspection, our restaurant fire inspection checklist helps you spot common problems.

Tell Us Where Your Kitchen Is

When you send a request, include the street address or ZIP code and, if you know it, the fire department or district that inspects you. If a fire inspector has already written you up, include the notice and its deadline so the provider knows the timeline. If you're in a stadium, casino, hospital or campus kitchen with its own facilities team, mention who should be contacted to schedule access. The more specific the location, the easier it is for the provider to plan around your AHJ's permit and acceptance steps.

Health department food permits are a separate track in every region. Passing a health inspection doesn't mean your hood system has been reviewed by the fire authority.

Not sure which region or fire district you're in? Just give us your kitchen's ZIP code in the request form. The provider who contacts you can confirm your fire authority.

FAQ

Service Area Questions

Which areas do you accept requests from?

Restaurants and commercial kitchens in St. Louis City, St. Louis County, St. Charles County in Missouri, and the Metro East in Illinois, including Belleville, Edwardsville, O'Fallon and Collinsville.

How do I find out who my fire authority is?

In St. Louis City it's the St. Louis Fire Department. Elsewhere it's the city fire department or fire protection district that covers your address, which doesn't always match your mailing city. Ask your city hall, or have the provider confirm it.

Is there one fire code for the whole St. Louis area?

No. Missouri has no statewide fire code for restaurants, so editions range from the 2015 to the 2021 IFC depending on the city or district. Illinois adds a statewide Life Safety Code (NFPA 101, 2015) on top of local codes.

Do you cover St. Charles, Illinois?

No. We cover St. Charles County, Missouri. St. Charles, Illinois is in the Chicago area and outside our service area.

Does it cost anything to send a request?

No. Submitting a request is free. The independent provider that contacts you quotes its work directly to you.
