Kitchen Fire Suppression in St. Louis County

The same hood job can run through different offices and different code editions depending on which side of a district line your kitchen sits on. Here's how to figure out who your fire authority is and what they tend to ask for.

Short answer: St. Louis County has no single fire code for restaurants. It's served by 19 municipal fire departments and 24 fire protection districts, and each one adopts its own fire code and runs its own permits and inspections. Your authority having jurisdiction (AHJ) is whichever department or district covers your address, and code editions range from the 2015 to the 2021 International Fire Code.

One County, Dozens of Fire Authorities

In June 2026, Firehouse reported that St. Louis County "is currently served by 19 municipal fire departments, run by local cities, and 24 fire districts," with each district governed by an elected board. The same report covered seven cities (Olivette, University City, Clayton, Richmond Heights, Maplewood, Webster Groves and Shrewsbury) studying a possible fire department merger. Older counts differ slightly, and a 2015 study counted 43 departments and districts in all. The exact number shifts, but the practical point doesn't: your kitchen answers to one specific fire authority, and its rules may not match the one a mile away.

Fire district boundaries don't always follow city limits. A restaurant with a city mailing address may be protected by a fire protection district rather than a city fire department. If you're not sure who your AHJ is, ask your city hall or the fire department that responds to your address, or ask the provider to confirm it when they quote the job.

Code note: Section numbers depend on the edition your AHJ adopted. Under the 2021 IFC, kitchen suppression is §904.13 and hood maintenance is §606. Under the 2015 IFC, they're §904.12 and §609. If a write-up cites a section that doesn't seem to match, the edition is usually why.

The Metro West Discharge Test

One local amendment stands out. Metro West FPD's Ordinance No. 121, which adopts the 2021 IFC as the district's Fire Prevention Code, amends acceptance testing (§904.4.4) to say that "all dry-chemical and wet-chemical extinguishing systems shall also be tested by a discharge of the extinguishing agent," in addition to an expellant-gas discharge through the piping and nozzles. In plain terms, a new system in that district is expected to actually discharge agent during acceptance, which affects scheduling, cleanup and recharge planning before opening day. If you're building out a kitchen in the Wildwood or Ellisville area, raise this with your provider early. The kitchen fire suppression installation page explains the permit and acceptance steps in more detail.

What This Means for Your Kitchen

  • Routine service is the same everywhere. The IFC requires service at least every six months and after any activation. Fusible links are replaced at least every six months under NFPA 17A/NFPA 96 and manufacturer guidance; some code texts set an annual minimum. The kitchen fire suppression inspection page covers what a semiannual visit includes.
  • Permits come from your district or city. New systems, modifications and appliance changes go through the AHJ that covers your address, not a county office.
  • Write-ups follow local practice. Deadlines, re-inspection steps and how corrections are reported vary. If you were cited, the fire code deficiency repair page explains common items and documentation.
  • Discharges need a recharge before cooking. After any activation, the system needs service before that cook line goes back into use. See hood system recharge and repair.

Health permits run separately

St. Louis County Department of Public Health issues food permits under County Ordinance 22.724, and those permits are reported to be void on a change of ownership. That's a health rule, not a fire code rule. Whether a new owner needs anything from the fire district is up to that district, so ask. Either way, a new owner should get the last semiannual report and check the service tag. Our restaurant fire inspection checklist is a good starting point.

Where the County's Restaurant Kitchens Are

County dining is spread across older downtowns and newer commercial corridors:

  • Clayton: the county seat, with steakhouses and business dining.
  • Maplewood: Manchester Road and Sutton Boulevard.
  • Kirkwood and Webster Groves: historic downtown restaurant districts.
  • Delmar Loop in University City, and the Brentwood and Richmond Heights corridor.
  • Chesterfield and Chesterfield Valley in west county, plus the Manchester Road corridor.
  • Casinos: Hollywood Casino in Maryland Heights and River City Casino in Lemay.
  • Campuses: UMSL in Normandy and Washington University's Danforth Campus on the Clayton and University City line.

Recent Kitchen Fires in the County

Two recent fires show why hood areas get so much attention from fire officials:

  • Three Kings Public House, Delmar Loop (April 26, 2023). A three-alarm fire around 3 a.m. University City Fire Battalion Chief Jon Bollinger said "the fire started in the hood vent area of the kitchen."
  • Asador Del Sur, Maplewood (August 30, 2025). Firefighters believed the fire started in the kitchen area. No injuries were reported.

Neither report says whether a suppression system operated. What they show is that the hood and kitchen area is where restaurant fires tend to start, which is what semiannual service and hood cleaning are meant to address.

Neighboring Areas

East of the county line, St. Louis City has a single fire authority on the 2018 IFC. Across the Missouri River, St. Charles County, Missouri fire districts have largely moved to the 2021 IFC and use occupancy permits. Across the Mississippi, Metro East, Illinois adds state rules and technician licensing.

FAQ

St. Louis County Hood Suppression Questions

Which fire district inspects my restaurant?

Whichever municipal fire department or fire protection district covers your address. District lines don't always match city limits, so ask your city hall or the fire department that responds to your address if you're unsure.

Does every St. Louis County fire district use the same code?

No. Each one adopts its own. Metro West FPD and Clayton use the 2021 IFC, while Mehlville FPD's policy book still cites the 2015 IFC in most policies. Ask your AHJ which edition and local amendments apply.

What is a discharge acceptance test?

It's a test where the new system actually releases its wet chemical agent so the fire official can see it work. Metro West FPD's Ordinance No. 121 requires wet- and dry-chemical systems to be tested by a discharge of the agent at acceptance. Plan cleanup and a recharge into the schedule.

Does Clayton require a permit for hood suppression work?

Yes. Clayton lists kitchen hood systems among its fire protection permits, handled by the Clayton Fire Department. The permit card and approved plans must be on site, or the inspection request is rejected. Food trucks operating in Clayton also need a fire operational permit, so confirm current requirements with Clayton FD.

I'm buying an existing restaurant. Do I need a new fire inspection?

The County health department's food permit is reported to be void on a change of ownership, but fire requirements depend on your district. Ask the district, and get the last semiannual service report so you know the system's condition before you open.

How often does a hood suppression system need service in the county?

At least every six months and after any discharge, under the IFC and NFPA 96, no matter which district you're in. Your district may add its own reporting rules.
