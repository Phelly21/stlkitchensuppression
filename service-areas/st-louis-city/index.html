Restaurant Fire Suppression in St. Louis City

One fire department, one code edition, and a lot of kitchens packed into a few square miles. Here's how hood suppression rules work inside the City of St. Louis, and how to get a request to a provider who serves it.

Short answer: In the City of St. Louis, the St. Louis Fire Department's Bureau of Fire Prevention enforces the fire code, and it reviews fire protection system plans together with the City's Building Division. Who Enforces the Fire Code in the City

Unlike St. Louis County, where dozens of separate departments and districts each set their own rules, the City has a single authority having jurisdiction (AHJ) for fire code questions: the St. Louis Fire Department's Bureau of Fire Prevention, which operates under the Fire Marshal. The Bureau has three divisions. Code Enforcement and Fire Inspections is described by the City as "the regulatory arm of the Bureau," and it works with the Building Division's Fire Safety Unit.

Two details matter for restaurant owners:

  • Plan review is shared. The City says plan review of fire protection systems is done together with the Building Division. If you're adding a hood, changing the cook line, or replacing a system, expect both offices to be involved.
  • Permits start at City Hall. The Building Division's Permit Section (City Hall, Room 425, 1200 Market St.) takes commercial permit applications and routes drawings to the reviewing sections and agencies.

We could not find a published, hood-specific fire permit form or fee for the City. Don't assume one does or doesn't exist. Ask the Bureau of Fire Prevention (314-289-1960) or the Permit Section what your particular project needs, or have your provider confirm it before work starts.

Which Code Edition Applies

As of our research, City kitchens are still governed by the 2018 cycle of the International Codes, while many St. Louis County districts and St. Charles County have moved to the 2021 editions.

Why the edition matters: section numbers moved between cycles. A checklist written for a 2021-IFC jurisdiction will cite §904.13 for kitchen suppression and §606 for hoods. In the City, the matching sections are 2018 IFC §904.12 and §607. The substance is similar. The 2018-cycle text requires the system to be serviced at least every six months and after any activation, by qualified individuals. Common Touchpoints for City Kitchens

Routine semiannual service

Most of what a City restaurant needs is the twice-a-year visit: testing the detection line and pull station, confirming the gas or electric shutoff works, checking nozzles and caps, and replacing fusible links. Fusible links are replaced at least every six months under NFPA 17A/NFPA 96 and manufacturer guidance; some code texts set an annual minimum. See what a visit covers on the kitchen fire suppression inspection page, or the ANSUL system service page if your cylinder says ANSUL R-102 or Piranha.

When the system is impaired

The Fire Department publishes fire-watch requirements, citing Ordinance 70796 and the 2018 IFC, for times when fire protection systems are impaired. If your hood system discharged, failed its test, or was red-tagged, ask the Bureau whether a fire watch applies to you and don't cook under that hood until the system is restored. A provider can handle the hood system recharge and repair, and our guide to what to do after an Ansul system goes off covers cleanup in the meantime.

Remodels and new kitchens

Moving a fryer, adding a charbroiler, or converting a storefront into a restaurant can mean new or modified suppression coverage and a trip through joint plan review. The kitchen fire suppression installation page walks through design, permits, and acceptance testing.

Health permits are a separate track

The City Department of Health's guide to opening a food business requires a Building Division occupancy permit, but it doesn't mention the Fire Department or hood suppression. Passing a health inspection doesn't mean your hood system has been reviewed. Keep the two tracks separate in your opening checklist.

Got a fire inspection write-up? Note the deadline and the exact wording on the notice, then send a request. Our fire code deficiency repair page explains what usually gets cited and how corrections are documented.

Where the City's Commercial Kitchens Are

The City's restaurant density is concentrated in a handful of districts, and several large venues run multiple kitchens under one roof:

  • Dining districts: The Hill (the city's Italian neighborhood), Soulard (corner pubs and Creole and Cajun spots), Cherokee Street (Mexican and Latino restaurants), South Grand near Tower Grove Park, the Central West End's sidewalk cafes, The Grove, Benton Park, Lafayette Square, Downtown and Washington Avenue, and Laclede's Landing on the riverfront.
  • Stadiums and arenas: Busch Stadium, Enterprise Center, and Energizer Park, the CITY SC stadium in Downtown West (renamed from CITYPARK effective January 1, 2025, with 22,423 seats). Venues like these often have many hoods across concession stands, clubs, and commissary kitchens, so service is usually planned around event calendars.
  • Casino: Horseshoe St. Louis (formerly Lumière Place) on the downtown riverfront.
  • Institutions: Saint Louis University in Midtown and the Washington University Medical Campus in the Central West End, where dining halls and hospital kitchens operate on tight schedules.

A Reminder From The Hill

Kitchen exhaust fires aren't hypothetical here. In 2009, a fire damaged Zia's on The Hill. According to reports at the time, investigators ruled it accidental and found it started around the grease vent in the kitchen hood. That's the area duct and plenum nozzles are designed to protect.

Food Trucks in the City

Food trucks that cook with grease fall under the fire code's mobile food vehicle section (2018 IFC §319), which points back to §904.12 for automatic suppression. The City runs an annual fire registration and inspection for food trucks through its Fire Safety Unit. Confirm the current form and schedule with the City before your inspection. Our food truck fire suppression page covers what trucks typically need.

What to Have Ready When You Request Service

  • Your address and whether you're in a stadium, hotel, casino, or other host building with its own facilities team.
  • The system brand and model from the cylinder label, plus your last service tag date.
  • Any Fire Department notice, including the deadline and inspector's contact.
  • Whether appliances have been added, moved, or swapped since the last service.

Pricing depends on the number of hoods, appliances and nozzles, the system brand, the parts replaced, and scheduling. Our restaurant fire suppression cost guide explains how to compare quotes.

Neighboring Areas

The City of St. Louis is an independent city, separate from St. Louis County, where the fire authority depends on which municipal department or fire protection district covers your address. Across the river, Metro East, Illinois layers state rules and state technician licensing on top of local codes. Farther west, St. Charles County, Missouri runs on the 2021 IFC and fire-district occupancy permits.

FAQ

St. Louis City Hood Suppression Questions

Who enforces the fire code for restaurants in the City of St. Louis?

The St. Louis Fire Department's Bureau of Fire Prevention, under the Fire Marshal. Its Code Enforcement and Fire Inspections division works with the Building Division's Fire Safety Unit, and fire protection system plans are reviewed jointly with the Building Division.

Which fire code edition does the City use?

The 2018 International Fire Code, adopted by Ordinance 70796 and later amended by Ordinance 71844 (effective June 6, 2024). Kitchen suppression requirements are in 2018 IFC §904.12, and hood requirements are in §607.

Is the City of St. Louis part of St. Louis County?

No. The City is an independent city with its own fire department. If your restaurant is in Clayton, Maplewood, or another county municipality, see the St. Louis County page, because your fire authority is different.

Does passing a health inspection cover my hood suppression system?

No. Health permits and inspections come from the City Department of Health and focus on food safety. Hood suppression falls under the fire code, so keep your semiannual service reports and tag current separately.

Do food trucks need a fire inspection in the City?

Yes. The City runs an annual fire registration and inspection for food trucks through its Fire Safety Unit, and trucks that cook with grease need automatic suppression under 2018 IFC §319. Confirm the current form with the City, and see our food truck fire suppression page.

How often does a City restaurant's hood system need service?

At least every six months and after any discharge, under the 2018 IFC and NFPA 96. Service should also follow a major hood and duct cleaning, since links are sometimes wired shut during cleaning.
