Restaurant Hood Fire Suppression in the Metro East, Illinois

Illinois adds a state layer that Missouri doesn't have: a state Life Safety Code, state-adopted NFPA editions for fire equipment service, and a state license for the technicians who do the work. Here's how it fits together with Belleville, Edwardsville and other local fire departments.

  • NFPA 101 (2015) statewide
  • Illinois Class 2K licensing
  • Witnessed wet function tests

Short answer: Metro East restaurants answer to two layers: the Illinois Office of the State Fire Marshal (OSFM), which adopted the 2015 NFPA 101 Life Safety Code statewide, and their city fire department, which enforces its own adopted fire code. Belleville and Edwardsville both require a permit for new or modified hood suppression systems that includes an inspector witnessing a wet function test. Illinois also requires technicians who service these systems to hold a state license.

The State Layer: Illinois OSFM

Unlike Missouri, Illinois has a statewide fire safety code. The OSFM adopted the NFPA 101 Life Safety Code, 2015 edition, into 41 Ill. Adm. Code 100, effective January 1, 2020. State law (the Fire Investigation Act) requires local fire officers to enforce the state's adopted fire safety rules, so this applies in every Metro East town on top of local codes.

A separate state rule governs fire equipment servicing. As amended effective October 26, 2023, 41 Ill. Adm. Code 251.25 adopts NFPA 17A (2021), NFPA 96 (2021) and NFPA 10 (2022) for that work. Those editions are fixed in the rule, so a newer NFPA edition doesn't apply until Illinois updates it.

Free, voluntary state plan review

The OSFM offers plan review for special extinguishing systems, including kitchen fire suppression systems. In its own words, this review "is not mandatory by any state statute or administrative rule," and the services "are currently conducted without charge." The OSFM reviews only against state-adopted codes, mainly NFPA 101, so you still have to meet local rules, which may be stricter. Results are copied to the local fire chief.

The OSFM's Hood & Duct submittal form is worth knowing about even if you never file it. It treats the hood, duct, exhaust fan, grease removal devices and suppression system as one system, each reviewed separately. It covers converting an existing dry chemical system to a liquid (wet) chemical system and asks whether the system complies with UL 300, NFPA 96 and NFPA 17A. It also notes that if you upgrade only the suppression system, the owner or contractor remains responsible for the existing hood and duct meeting the codes in force when they were installed. Our UL 300 compliance guide explains why older dry-chemical systems get replaced.

Who Can Service a Hood System in Illinois

Illinois requires a license for this work. Under the OSFM's fire equipment program, a Class 2K Fire Equipment Employee license covers people who "service, recharge, hydro-test, install, maintain, or inspect all types of pre-engineered kitchen fire extinguishing systems." It requires ICC or NAFED certification in pre-engineered kitchen fire extinguishing systems, and the 2K employee works for a Class B licensed distributor under 41 Ill. Adm. Code 251. Confirm current rules with the Office of the State Fire Marshal.

For you, that means one extra question before work starts: ask the provider for the technician's Illinois license and the company's distributor license. A company based on the Missouri side has to meet the Illinois rules for work done in Illinois.

Routine Service and Write-Ups

Semiannual service applies here too: NFPA 96 and the IFC call for service at least every six months and after any discharge. Fusible links are replaced at least every six months under NFPA 17A/NFPA 96 and manufacturer guidance; some code texts set an annual minimum. The kitchen fire suppression inspection page covers a typical visit. If a fire inspector or a business license review flagged your system, see fire code deficiency repair. After a discharge, you'll need a hood system recharge before cooking. Food permits come from the St. Clair County and Madison County health departments and are separate from fire permits.

Recent Kitchen Fires in the Metro East

  • Hardee's, Glen Carbon (June 16, 2025). Glen Carbon Fire Chief Jason Whitaker said, "It was contained inside the hood system." Glen Carbon, Edwardsville and Maryville responded, and the health department planned to inspect the restaurant and its hood system.
  • Captain D's, Edwardsville (March 4, 2026). The cause was reported as undetermined, and smoke and fire damage kept the location from reopening right away.

A fire kept inside the hood is the outcome hood protection is designed for, and it depends on a system that has been serviced and recharged on schedule.

FAQ

Metro East Hood Suppression Questions

Who can service a restaurant hood suppression system in Illinois?

Illinois requires an OSFM Class 2K Fire Equipment Employee license, which calls for ICC or NAFED certification in pre-engineered kitchen fire extinguishing systems, and the technician works for a Class B licensed distributor under 41 Ill. Adm. Code 251. Confirm current rules with the Office of the State Fire Marshal.

Do I need a permit for a new or modified hood system in Belleville?

Yes. Belleville FD requires a Kitchen Hood Suppression permit for any new system, modification or upgrade. It covers plan review, hood and duct rough-in, clean-outs, fuel interlocks and one witness of a full wet function test. Work without a permit is charged at 200% of the fee.

Does the fire department watch the system discharge?

In Belleville and Edwardsville, yes. Both cities' hood suppression permits include an inspector witnessing one wet function test at acceptance, so plan for cleanup and a recharge before you open.

Is OSFM plan review required for a kitchen suppression system?

No. The OSFM says plan review of kitchen fire suppression systems is not mandatory under state statute or rule and is currently free. It checks only state-adopted codes, and results are copied to your local fire chief, who may require more.

Which code applies in the Metro East, state or city?

Both. The state's NFPA 101 (2015) Life Safety Code applies everywhere, and your city enforces its own adopted fire code, such as the 2015 IFC in Belleville or the 2021 IFC in Edwardsville.

Can a Missouri fire protection company work on my Illinois kitchen?

Only if it meets Illinois licensing for the work. We found no Missouri statewide license for kitchen suppression technicians, but Illinois has one, so ask any provider for its Illinois distributor license and the technician's Class 2K license.
